       \item We claim that $\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k(k+1)} = \frac{n}{n+1}, , \forall n\in \mathbb{Z}$. We provide two different proofs. \begin{proof}
        First, we can proceed via algebraic manipulation. We find that $\frac{1}{k(k+1)} = \frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{k+1}$. Rewriting, we find that the sum of these terms is a basic telescopic series: \begin{center}
            $\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(\frac{1}{k} - \frac{1}{k+1})=(\frac{1}{1} - \frac{1}{2})+(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3})+(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{4})\ldots (\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{n+1})$.
        \end{center} We see that every term cancels with its opposite, other than $1$ and $\frac{1}{n+1}$. Thus, $\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(\frac{1}{k} - \frac{1}{k+1})=1-\frac{1}{n+1}=\frac{n}{n+1}$.
       \end{proof}

       \begin{proof}
        Another method of proof is by induction.
